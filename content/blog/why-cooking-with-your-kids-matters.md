There's a reason we chose the kitchen. It's not because cooking is the most important skill a kid can learn — although it's a good one. It's because the kitchen creates the right conditions for connection.

## The hands are busy

When you're chopping vegetables or stirring a pot, the pressure of eye contact goes away. Kids — especially older ones — talk more freely when they're doing something with their hands that isn't their phone. The kitchen gives them that cover.

## The stakes are low

A burned pancake isn't a crisis. A spilled measuring cup isn't a disaster. The kitchen is a place where mistakes are small and fixable, and that makes it a safe place to practice — both cooking and talking.

## The payoff is immediate

You cook together, you eat together, you clean up together. The whole arc happens in one evening. There's a beginning, a middle, and an end. Kids get to see the result of their effort on the table in front of them.

## It's repeatable

This isn't a one-time event. It's Tuesday. It's Thursday. It's whenever you have an hour and a recipe. The more you do it, the more natural the conversation becomes — and the more your kids expect it and look forward to it.

## Start small

You don't need a complicated recipe. You don't need a fancy kitchen. You need a meal, a kid, and a question worth asking. That's it.
